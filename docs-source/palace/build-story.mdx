The Sovereign Markdown Palace was not planned from the beginning. It was built in response to a real problem that emerged after six months of daily AI use — a problem that will eventually affect every DSOM project if the brain is allowed to grow without spatial structure. Understanding why the Palace was built helps you use it correctly and avoid the same pitfalls that triggered its creation.

## The Problem: When the Drawer Becomes Unmanageable

The original DSOM memory model was elegant in its simplicity: every session's Mental Anchor and decision log was written into `walkthrough.md`. The file grew chronologically, and the AI read it at Start of Day to reconstruct context.

This worked well for the first few months. Then it stopped working.

After six months of daily sessions, `walkthrough.md` had grown to hundreds of lines. The SOD manifest — generated by `reanimate.sh` and uploaded to the AI — had bloated to an unmanageable token count. Three distinct failure modes appeared:

- **Context Decay:** The AI would miss architectural decisions buried deep in the log because its effective attention faded over the earlier sections when later sections were dominant.
- **Attention Drift:** Loading 300+ lines of session history caused the AI to over-weight recent entries and under-weight earlier ones — even when the earlier ones were more architecturally important.
- **Linear Retrieval:** There was no way to ask "what do we know about Ansible inventory structure?" without either reading everything or relying on the AI's increasingly unreliable recall of a document it had only partially processed.

The flat file model was excellent for *persistence* — nothing was lost. It was weak for *retrieval* — finding the right thing at the right time.

## The Insight: Method of Loci for AI Memory

The solution came from an ancient memorisation technique. Greek and Roman orators used the **Method of Loci** — also known as the Memory Palace — to memorise entire speeches by mentally placing ideas in specific rooms of an imaginary building. To recall an idea, the orator mentally walked to the room where they had placed it. The spatial structure of the building made retrieval effortless.

The DSOM adaptation was direct: stop asking the AI to read everything in sequence, and start letting it *walk* to what it needs.

The insight was that `walkthrough.md` (the Drawer) should remain untouched as the verbatim temporal record — the full book. What was missing was the index: a spatial layer that organised the book's contents into navigable rooms so the AI could go to the right shelf instead of reading every page.

This insight was crystallised by the [MemPalace project](https://github.com/milla-jovovich/mempalace), which demonstrated that AI memory systems benefit from the same spatial organisation principles that human memory experts have used for millennia. The DSOM Palace is directly inspired by and originated from the MemPalace approach.

## The Two-Layer Architecture

The Palace was designed as a second layer *alongside* the Drawer, never as a replacement for it. This became the **Dual-Mode Persistence** model:

| Dimension | The Drawer (`walkthrough.md`) | The Palace (`wings/`) |
|---|---|---|
| **Logic** | Temporal — time-ordered | Semantic — meaning-ordered |
| **Role** | Persistence: the permanent record | Recall: the fast retrieval path |
| **Storage** | Single flat file | Structured directory tree |
| **Usage** | Archival audit trail | Active session reanimation |

The Drawer answers "what happened?" The Palace answers "what does the AI need right now?" Both questions need answers, and neither layer answers both.

## The closet.md Concept

The key innovation of the Palace is the `closet.md` — a distilled, high-density knowledge summary that lives in every Room. Instead of feeding the AI a 300-line session log, you feed it a 30-line closet that contains 95% of the relevant context in 5% of the tokens.

A well-written closet holds:

- The essential facts and decisions for that topic
- A timeline of how the topic evolved across sessions
- A retrieval reference linking back to the exact line range in `walkthrough.md` where the original information lives

The cross-link to the Drawer is what prevents AI hallucination. The closet is a navigational shortcut, not a standalone source of truth. When the AI needs precision it cannot find in the closet, it follows the link into the full Drawer entry.

The result is measurable. With the Palace in place, AI context reconstruction at Start of Day dropped from thousands of tokens (full `walkthrough.md` scan) to approximately 50 tokens of Palace navigation plus targeted closet reads.

## The Reasoning Gap: What the Palace Does Not Solve

The Palace solved the state persistence and retrieval problem effectively. But the research finding recorded on 2026-04-08 identified a second gap that the Palace does not address: **live reasoning capture**.

Six months of daily AI use generates millions of tokens of conversation. Every decision, every debugging session, and every tradeoff debate lives in those conversations. The Palace captures *what* was decided and *what* was built. It does not automatically capture *why* — the reasoning, the alternatives that were rejected, and the logic behind each choice.

The reasoning lives in the chat window. When the session ends, it is gone.

<Note>
  **The Reasoning Gap (Research Finding 2026-04-08):** DSOM solves state persistence via the Sovereign Palace, but does not automatically capture live reasoning. Decisions are recorded; their rationale is not — unless you explicitly capture it.
</Note>

## The Fix: Decision Log Protocol

<Tip>
  At every significant decision point — an architecture choice, a tool selection, a tradeoff — ask your AI to log the reasoning immediately before the session ends:

  ```text
  Log this decision to walkthrough.md:
  Decision: [what was decided]
  Alternatives rejected: [what else was considered and why it was not chosen]
  Reason: [why this decision was made]
  ```

  This single habit closes the Reasoning Gap. The decision log entry goes into the Drawer, which means the Palace can reference it from the relevant Room's closet. The *what* and the *why* are now both sovereign and version-controlled.
</Tip>

The Decision Log Protocol is the recommended practice for closing the Reasoning Gap in any long-running DSOM project. Apply it consistently from day one to keep both the *what* and the *why* sovereign and version-controlled.
